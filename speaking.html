The Three Body Problem: Building Agency in a World of Complexity

Individuals, organizations, and societies are all facing the same problem. What if the way through is the same, too?

Keynote · 30–60 min Workshop & cohort formats available Leaders & practitioners

Rising complexity is breaking our operating systems at every scale: the individual drowning in demands they didn’t choose, the organization that can’t change how it works, the society straining under problems its institutions weren’t built for. We treat these as three separate crises with three separate bookshelves. This talk—drawn from the course Sam taught at Critical Business School—argues they’re the same problem at different scales, and that the way through isn’t more control. It’s building genuine agency: the ability to see the system you’re inside and act on it deliberately.

Audiences leave with

  • A way of seeing their personal, organizational, and societal challenges as one problem at three scales
  • The “operating system” lens: what it means to upgrade how you work, not just what you work on
  • Concrete starting points for building agency inside systems they didn’t design

Organizational Catalysts

Everyone owns a piece of how your organization works. Who owns the system?

Keynote · 30–60 min Workshop format available HR, OD & transformation leaders

Most organizations are renting their change capability from consultants when they should be building their own. The old assumption of “design once, run forever” has been replaced by the reorg treadmill: restructure, wait eighteen months, restructure again. This talk makes the case for a different function entirely—an organizational catalyst that owns the operating system and tends its ongoing fitness. Drawing on an extended detour through nuclear physics—fission to release the energy trapped in organizational debt, fusion to bring fragmented capability together—it argues the goal isn’t a changed state. It’s criticality: a self-maintaining capacity for adaptation.

Audiences leave with

  • A diagnosis of why both the reorg treadmill and rented transformation fail—and the middle ground between them
  • The fission/fusion lens for spotting trapped energy: organizational debt to release, fragmentation to fuse
  • Three moves for making the case without formal authority—and why AI makes this function urgent now

Facilitation Is Work Design

If culture is created by the quality of interactions—and meetings are where most interactions happen—what are your meetings building?

Keynote · 30–60 min Best as an interactive session Facilitators, team leads & practitioners

Facilitation gets reduced to tips and tricks for running a meeting. This talk reframes it as something much bigger: meeting design as the most accessible form of work design there is. Meetings amplify and reflect the organization back to us—who gets invited reveals assumptions about authority, whether decisions stick reveals whether authority is clear. Which makes better meetings a helpful Trojan horse: improving them quickly surfaces the deeper questions about strategy, decision rights, and ways of working.

Audiences leave with

  • The core reframe: to change your organization, go to where the interactions are and make them better
  • The three meeting modes—diverge, converge, align—and the discipline of doing one at a time
  • A facilitator’s real job description: holding the container, plus five facilitative skills to develop
